## 1. The reference variety probe carries three measurement defects

This is the most consequential finding. The probe's headline result, quoted in
this repository as *"on the two consonants where the varieties genuinely differ,
the rhotic and t, Australian speakers were flagged about three points more often
under the American reference"*, does not survive audit.

Published numbers were reproduced exactly from the 2,400 per clip evidence files
before anything was questioned, so the decomposition below rests on the same data
that produced `speech_sound_patterns/variety-probe-v1.0.0.json`.

### 1a. Five palatal classes are unscoreable and nobody noticed

Flag rates under the American reference at threshold minus one, all groups pooled:

| phone | opportunities | flag rate |
|---|---|---|
| `ʎ` | 857 | 1.0000 |
| `ɟ` | 308 | 1.0000 |
| `c` | 419 | 1.0000 |
| `ç` | 612 | 0.9984 |
| `ɲ` | 760 | 0.9947 |

**These five classes account for 38.9 percent of every flag the probe produces.**
They are flagged at essentially 100 percent in the American control as well as in
the comparison groups, which is the signature of a phone the model never emits
rather than of a pronunciation error.

The probe's `without_conditioned_palatals` secondary analysis excludes
`{c, cʰ, ɟ, ʎ, ɲ}` and **misses `ç`**, which is 612 opportunities at 99.84 percent.

**The repair already exists in this repository.** The normalisation table at
`speech_sound_patterns/prompt_pack.py` lines 115 to 145, built one checkpoint later
for the research prompt pack, maps every one of these to a broad phoneme with a
written reason: `c` and `ɟ` to the velar stops, `ɲ` to `n`, `ç` to `h`, `ʎ` to `l`.
It was never applied back to the probe, which still scores all five raw in
`SCORABLE_CONSONANTS` at `speech_sound_patterns/variety_reference.py` line 124.

The same file's own comment states the correct test and shows it was applied once
and only once: *"Dark l is absent: nothing maps to it since mapping version 1.1.0,
because the model never emits it."* Applying that test across the whole inventory
catches all five.

### 1b. Pre consonantal coda rhotic is unscoreable, and it manufactures the headline effect

Splitting `ɹ` by position under the American reference, threshold minus one:

| group | pre consonantal | pre vocalic | word final |
|---|---|---|---|
| American control | **0.9660** (n=470) | 0.0769 (n=1405) | 0.4583 (n=72) |
| Australian | **0.9665** (n=269) | 0.0843 (n=700) | 0.4773 (n=44) |
| British | **0.9662** (n=266) | 0.0871 (n=746) | 0.4000 (n=35) |

Pre consonantal coda `ɹ` is flagged 96.6 percent of the time **in every group,
identical to three decimal places, including rhotic American speakers who
unambiguously produce it**. This is a broken opportunity class, not an accent
penalty.

The reported Australian rhotic differential of +0.0300 therefore reflects prompt
composition, not speech. Australian prompts carry 269 of 1,013 rhotic
opportunities in pre consonantal position (26.6 percent) against the American
control's 470 of 1,947 (24.1 percent). That 2.5 point difference in opportunity
composition, multiplied by a 96.6 percent flag rate everyone receives, accounts
for roughly 2.4 of the 3.0 points.

This class could never have been caught by the normalisation table, because coda
`ɹ` is the same symbol as onset `ɹ`. It is positionally unmatchable rather than
lexically wrong. That is a genuinely distinct failure mode and it is the most
publishable single observation in this repository.

The cause is established as a fact and **not** as a mechanism. Whether it is a
phone mapping error, a limitation of the segmentation free GOP formulation, or a
real weakness of the acoustic model is unresolved and needs diagnosis before
anything is written.

### 1c. Nothing survives speaker clustered uncertainty

The probe computes means and differences only. A search of every item 22 variety
probe file for bootstrap, confidence interval, p value, mixed effects, standard
error, permutation or multiple comparison correction returns no hit.

Speaker clustered bootstrap, 2,000 resamples, threshold minus one:

| comparison | point | 95 percent CI | crosses zero |
|---|---|---|---|
| AU minus US, US reference, group | −0.0035 | [−0.0164, +0.0086] | yes |
| GB minus US, US reference, group | +0.0115 | [−0.0024, +0.0265] | yes |
| GB minus US, UK reference, group | +0.0053 | [−0.0091, +0.0204] | yes |
| rhotic AU minus US, US reference | +0.0300 | [−0.0027, +0.0639] | yes |
| `t` AU minus US, US reference | +0.0298 | [−0.0001, +0.0603] | yes |

**Every headline differential's interval includes zero**, before any correction
across roughly thirty consonants, two references and five thresholds. This
includes the per consonant effect that `variety_probe_validate.py` currently
requires to be positive, so that validator will need changing for an honest
report to pass.

There is also an aggregation inconsistency: group rates are computed per speaker
then averaged, while the headline per consonant finding pools token counts and has
no speaker clustering at all. The rhotic sits at about 3.4 tokens per speaker.

### 1d. What remains after the defects are removed

Recomputed per speaker, threshold minus one:

| analysis | base rate | AU minus US | GB minus US |
|---|---|---|---|
| as published | 0.1664 | −0.0035 | +0.0115 |
| palatals removed | 0.1096 | −0.0024 | +0.0087 |
| palatals and pre consonantal coda rhotic removed | 0.0909 | −0.0035 | +0.0071 |

The base flag rate falls by 45 percent. The Australian differential stays negative
throughout, so the original failed prediction remains failed. The British
differential shrinks and, per the intervals above, is not distinguishable from
zero.

### 1e. Two further weaknesses a reviewer will raise

- **Prompt sets are effectively disjoint across groups.** Australian speakers read
  592 unique prompts, American speakers 1,161, and the intersection is 34. The
  design is paired across references, on the same clip and the same inference, but
  entirely unmatched across groups, so every group comparison confounds variety
  with lexical material. This is not among the six declared confounds.
- **Training lineage overlaps the evaluation data.** The scoring model
  `facebook/wav2vec2-lv-60-espeak-cv-ft` is fine tuned on Common Voice and the
  probe evaluates entirely on Common Voice 26 speakers.
  `speech_sound_patterns/engineering-plan.md` line 71 disqualifies Wav2Vec2
  CommonPhone for precisely this reason. The rule was never applied here.

### 1f. Cost to repair

Cheaper than it looks, because nothing needs re acquiring. The 2,400 decoded
16 kHz clips are retained at
`.research_data/speech_sound_patterns/variety-probe/canonical/` (233 MB) and the
per clip, per target evidence with hashed participant identifiers and both
references scored against the same posteriors is at `.../variety-probe/evidence/`
(9.8 MB).

- Recomputing published summaries by **dropping** broken classes: no re inference,
  minutes. This is how the decomposition above was produced.
- **Substituting** the palatals to their broad phonemes, which is the correct fix
  rather than the cheap one, changes the expected token sequence and therefore
  needs the model re run. Scoring reads frame posteriors, and only the per target
  scores were retained, not the posteriors themselves.
- Coda rhotic diagnosis: the same re run answers it.
- Full uncertainty quantification: from the re run's evidence, no further passes.

So the whole repair costs **one** pass over the 2,400 retained clips, not several.
Measure the per clip rate before quoting a duration; the runbook's 2.5 hour figure
predates these changes and an exploratory decode on 2026-08-23 ran considerably
slower per clip than that implies.

## 2. Licensing

### 2a. The commercial constraint was already void

`praat-parselmouth` is **GPL 3.0 or later** and is a hard runtime dependency of
the shipping acoustics stage, imported by `pipeline/acoustic_primitives.py` and
`pipeline/acoustics.py`.

Meanwhile `speech_sound_patterns/provider_register/provider-register-v1.2.0.json`
line 838 rejects a research tool with this reason, verbatim:

> "GPL-3.0 copyleft is incompatible with this proprietary commercial product and
> training provenance is opaque"

The project rejected Allosaurus for the exact licence it already depended on.
Commercial cleanliness was enforced ruthlessly on data and not at all on code, and
the data side is where it cost the evidence.

### 2b. The decision

**GPL 3.0 or later, whole repository.** Every other package in the 129 package
pinned closure is permissive or weak copyleft and is one way compatible into
GPLv3. Parselmouth being *or later* makes `GPL-3.0-or-later` the correct tag.

An Apache core with an isolated GPL acoustics component was considered and
rejected. The seam does already exist, because `run_all.py` invokes acoustics as a
subprocess reading `acoustics.json` and never imports it, and only
`regression/harness.py` line 20 breaks it with an in process import. But the split
is architecture work serving a legal constraint that no longer binds, for an
adoption benefit that does not apply to a research tool. Adam owns the copyright
and can dual licence later if anyone ever asks.

The linking question itself is genuinely unsettled in law. The FSF treats Python
bindings to a GPL library as creating a combined work; Lawrence Rosen argues no
form of linking does. No court has resolved it. Parselmouth is the harder case
either way, since it statically embeds Praat's GPL C++ into the wheel.

### 2c. What the direction change unlocks

Blocked on the non commercial clause and nothing else:

| source | licence | what it gives |
|---|---|---|
| Unisyn, CSTR Edinburgh | non commercial | the only source from which an Australian and American divergence list falls out mechanically; replaces the 5,347 word Wiktionary overlay |
| Mitchell and Delbridge | CC BY-NC 4.0 | 7,736 Australian speakers, 330 schools, every state and territory, two fixed consonant rich sentences each, downloadable anonymously |
| L2 ARCTIC | CC BY-NC 4.0 | manual phone level substitution, deletion and addition labels, 150 utterances per speaker |
| Buckeye | free, non commercial | hand corrected phone level labels, 40 first language English speakers, one city |
| Speech Accent Archive | CC BY-NC-SA 2.0 | narrow phonetic transcriptions across many first language backgrounds |
| MD_NLP, MAE VoiS, Sydney Speaks | non commercial | Australian speech |
| PhonBank and TalkBank | CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 | Phon format phonological transcription |

Still excluded regardless of the direction change, and the distinction matters:

- **CoANZSE** was force aligned with an American LibriSpeech acoustic model, so its
  alignment errors correlate with exactly the Australian and American differences
  this project measures. It would manufacture the bias it was brought in to detect.
  This is a methodological block, not a licence one.
- **AusTalk** has no access route at all. Every Alveo and AusTalk domain fails to
  resolve and it is not in LDaCA.
- **TIMIT** costs money and LDC's own materials speak only of organizations, so it
  may not be licensable to an unaffiliated individual at all. Confirm before
  planning any work that assumes it.

### 2d. What still does not exist

Only three corpora anywhere carry expert phone level pronunciation annotation:
SpeechOcean762, L2 ARCTIC and EpaDB. SpeechOcean762 is the only openly licensed one
and its speakers are all Mandarin first language, so it cannot support a claim about
English reference varieties. **There is no free, openly licensed corpus with expert
produced phone transcription spanning multiple English reference varieties.** No
engineering fixes this.

### 2e. Other licensing items

- There is **no `LICENSE` file** in the repository.
- `panphon` and `espnet2` are imported by `speech_sound_patterns` but declared in
  neither `requirements.txt` nor `constraints.txt`.
- `pyannote/speaker-diarization-3.1` weights are MIT but **gated**, requiring an
  account and token, so a fresh clone cannot run diarization turnkey. The solo path
  uses Silero VAD and has no such gate.
- The CC BY-SA lexicon derivatives must stay out of any release artifact.
  ShareAlike attaches when adapted material is distributed. The prompt pack already
  enforces `derived_lexicon_stays_server_side`.
- The PhoneticXEUS weights remain provenance unresolved and correctly quarantined.
- Nothing licence encumbered is committed. All corpora live in gitignored
  `.research_data/` and the manifests are metadata only.

## 3. Privacy and the publication route

### 3a. The repository cannot be published

Tracked personal material: three recordings under `audio/`, 31 files under
`output/` including full transcripts and evaluations, `history.json` and
`progress.md`. `output/a-whole-run.zip` is a binary duplicate of an entire
run that any extension based cleanup would miss.

Git history is worse than the working tree. Total 929 blobs, 28.4 MB.
`audio/the owner's conversation recording 2.m4a` at 3,064,693 bytes is the largest object in the
repository and exists **only** in history. Deleting from the tree achieves nothing.

The two speaker recording contains a second person. Adam confirmed on 2026-08-22
that they consented to being recorded. Consent to publication was not sought and is
a separate question under Invasion of Privacy Act 1971 (Qld) section 45, which the
repository's own regulatory reading already quotes. **The practical resolution is
that the recording simply never enters the public snapshot**, which removes the
question rather than answering it.

### 3b. The route

Sanitized snapshot, not a history rewrite. A rewrite changes all 73 commit hashes
anyway, so it preserves no history worth keeping, and it cannot prove completeness
if the remote was ever exposed. A fresh repository carries a clean provenance
guarantee that a rewrite cannot.

369 of 406 tracked files survive removal of `audio/`, `output/`, `history.json` and
`progress.md`. `regression/truth/*.json` hold only hashes and metadata, no
transcript content, so they survive, but they reference the personal audio by path
and need repointing at a synthetic fixture.

Before any first push: strip `the owner's home directory` from the ten tracked files
containing it including `README.md`, strip the account scoped AssemblyAI CDN URLs
and job identifiers from the transcripts, add a `LICENSE`, and replace the fixture
references in `README.md`, `AGENTS.md` and
`motor_speech_voice/engineering-plan.md`.

### 3c. Credential hygiene is clean

`.env` has never been tracked in any of the 73 commits. Every reachable Git object
was searched for the twelve literal live key values and returned nothing. Pattern
scans for the usual key shapes across the tree and all commits return nothing.
Apparent phone number matches are digit substrings inside floating point statistics
in `frozen-comparison-v1.0.0.json`.

## 4. Publication landscape

### 4a. Venues

- **Interspeech 2026 is in Sydney, 28 September to 1 October 2026**, theme
  "Diversity and Equity, Speaking Together". The submission deadline passed in
  February 2026. Verified against the ISCA upcoming conferences page.
- **Interspeech 2027**, Sao Paulo, 29 August to 2 September 2027. No call published
  yet; expect a deadline around February or March 2027 by the usual rhythm.
- **ICASSP 2027**, Toronto, submission 16 September 2026. Too soon for a first
  paper.
- **SLaTE** and **BEA** are the best fits: real peer review, ISCA Archive or ACL
  Anthology publication with a DOI, cheaper registration, and short paper formats.
  Neither 2027 edition is announced yet.
- Author registration at ICASSP runs 900 to 1,240 US dollars with **no student or
  independent researcher discount**. Interspeech is comparable.
- Neither conference requires an institutional affiliation.

### 4b. arXiv is harder than it used to be

arXiv changed its endorsement policy on 21 January 2026. Institutional email alone
no longer qualifies a new author, and the remaining path for an unaffiliated person
with no prior arXiv authorship is **personal endorsement from an established author
in the same endorsement domain**. Staff cannot waive it. `eess` and `cs` are
separate domains.

A second trap: since 31 October 2025 arXiv CS requires review articles and position
papers to have already passed peer review. A framework paper with no experiments
could plausibly be read that way in `cs.CL`.

### 4c. JOSS needs six months of public history

JOSS requires the repository to have been public for more than six months with
active development across that period, and explicitly names "all or most commits
concentrated in the last few weeks before submission" as grounds for desk
rejection. A desk rejection on those grounds is stated to be a not yet rather than
a never. **Publishing the sanitized snapshot starts that clock and costs nothing**,
so it should happen in parallel with the paper rather than after it.

### 4d. Novelty is narrower than this project assumed

Dialect fairness in speech is an active, populated area in 2026.

- **Koel Labs**, a funded startup whose stated mission is inclusive speech
  technology for all dialects, publishes open weights and training code and has a
  paper at Interspeech 2026 finding that G2P generated labels help only below 20 to
  30 hours of human annotation and beyond that reduce cross dialect robustness
  (arXiv 2606.16019).
- arXiv 2606.11639, 10 June 2026, audits phoneme based ASR for bias across accent,
  gender, ethnicity and age and proposes a substitution tolerant error metric.

Both must be read and cited.

The defensible contribution is narrower than "evidence guarded speech measurement":
**a reusable audit and abstention harness for pronunciation assessment**. Every
open source artifact in this space (GOPT, kaldi-gop, gop-pykaldi, Koel Labs) is a
model or a recipe. No widely used open audit harness exists. The coda rhotic
failure in section 1b is a precise demonstration of why one is needed.

What must **not** be claimed is the invention of evidence guarded machine learning.
Data Statements (Bender and Friedman 2018), Datasheets for Datasets, Model Cards,
Croissant and decades of selective prediction and conformal prediction literature
all precede it. The honest framing is first application to phone level speech
measurement with a working implementation.

## 5. Things worth keeping that this audit confirmed

Recorded because they are genuine strengths and should not be lost in a rewrite.

- The pre registration machinery is stronger than most published work in this area.
  The contract is a separate committed file, `variety_probe.load_contract()` refuses
  to run if any release boundary is open, and the validator fails the build if the
  failed Australian prediction is ever recorded as held.
- The experiment is genuinely reproducible: deterministic seeded sampling, CC0
  corpora, an Apache 2.0 model, CC BY 4.0 dictionaries, retained per clip evidence,
  and the superseded mapping version 1.0.0 run kept with its hash so the correction
  is auditable.
- The claim verification layer in `pipeline/verify.py` and
  `pipeline/claim_ledger.py` extracts every numeric claim from a language model
  written report and checks it against stored measurements, including sign and
  direction errors. This is a stronger and more topical contribution than the
  coaching scores it protects, and is a candidate for separate publication.
- Truth class separation, participant level splits, the corpus manifest fail closed
  validator, and the refusal to pool sources overlapping a candidate's training
  lineage are all methodologically sound and rare.
- The 0 to 99 CLARITY, WIT, WARMTH, PRESENCE and STORY scores in
  `pipeline/evaluate.py` are the opposite: language model output parsed by regular
  expression against hand written anchors. They are not validated measurement
  scales and must not appear in anything scientific.

## 6. Outcome of the repair, 2026-08-23

Mapping version 1.2.0. Six phone families corrected, the sample rebuilt, all 2,400
clips rescored in 3.55 hours. The rebuilt sample is byte identical in clip
selection, order, speakers, exclusions and seed, so the comparison below is
like for like. 68.3 percent of expected sequences changed, 3,277 of 4,800.

### Flag rates, threshold minus one, mean of per speaker rates

| group | American reference, before | after | British reference, before | after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| American control | 0.1664 | **0.0863** | 0.1378 | **0.0888** |
| Australian | 0.1629 | **0.0823** | 0.1306 | **0.0803** |
| British | 0.1779 | **0.0944** | 0.1431 | **0.0928** |

Roughly half of every flag was noise. Scoring opportunities fell only 1.5 percent,
from 22,350 to 22,012 in the control, because the palatals were renamed to their
broad phonemes rather than discarded. Opportunity counts are near identical across
the two references and the ratio is the same in all three groups, 1.034, 1.033 and
1.038, so no group is differentially disadvantaged by abstention.

### Differentials against the American control

| comparison | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Australian, American reference | −0.0035 | **−0.0039** |
| Australian, British reference | −0.0072 | **−0.0085** |
| British, American reference | +0.0115 | **+0.0081** |
| British, British reference | +0.0053 | **+0.0041** |

The Australian differential stays negative under both references and at every
threshold. The contract's central prediction failed before the correction and
fails slightly harder after it.

### The two retractions

**The rhotic effect does not exist.** Australian minus American on `ɹ` under the
American reference is now **−0.0004**, against **+0.0300** in the superseded
report. The old figure was pre consonantal coda r flagged at 96.6 percent in every
group, multiplied by prompt composition. The `ɹ` target now means onset r only.

**The recorded mechanism was itself an artifact.** Change under the reference swap:

| group | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| American control | −0.0286 | **+0.0025** |
| Australian | −0.0322 | **−0.0020** |
| British | −0.0348 | **−0.0015** |

The control moved before because post vocalic r was scored and always failed under
the American reference while the non rhotic reference stopped creating the
opportunity. With that segment excluded under both, the asymmetry is gone and the
control stays approximately in place, which is what the contract predicted. The
movements are about a fifth of a percentage point and support no claim.

### What survived

**The t differential.** Australian minus American on `t`: **+0.0269** under the
American reference, **+0.0037** under the British. Nearly unchanged from the
superseded +0.0298 and +0.0019. It is the largest single per consonant
differential in the comparison.

It is also not clearly separable from the noise floor. `v` runs **−0.0254** in the
opposite direction, with Australians flagged less often than the control, and
several other consonants sit at −0.010 to −0.019. Without speaker clustered
intervals and a correction for testing 25 consonants, `t` cannot be distinguished
from the spread. The report records its uncertainty state as `not_computed` and the
validator fails if that is edited away.

### Repository changes

- `speech_sound_patterns/variety-probe-v1.1.0.json` is the report. Version 1.0.0
  stays committed as the superseded record and **no longer validates**.
- Mapping 1.2.0 in `variety_reference.py`, with `POST_VOCALIC_RHOTIC_MERGES`,
  `MODEL_VOWELS`, `UNSCORABLE_PHONES` and a reduced `SCORABLE_CONSONANTS` of 25.
- The `without_conditioned_palatals` secondary analysis is retired. It excluded
  four of the five palatals and missed `ç`; the primary analysis now handles all
  six before a target is selected, so the filter would filter nothing.
- The validator refuses a returning retracted finding, a material rhotic effect, a
  report built on mapping 1.1.0, and any edit that claims uncertainty exists.
- Superseded evidence retained at `evidence-mapping-v1.1.0/` and
  `sample-mapping-v1.1.0.json`, alongside the existing 1.0.0 snapshots.
- 929 tests pass, up from 916.
